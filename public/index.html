Practice areas

The firm’s work is organized by the kind of property involved. Each list shows typical matters. If yours is not on it, ask.

Tract 1

Commercial

For owners, buyers, tenants, and developers of commercial property. The firm drafts and negotiates the contract, reviews title and survey, and sees the deal through closing.

Typical matters

  • Purchase and sale agreements
  • Office, retail, and industrial leases
  • Title commitment and survey review
  • Easements, access, and deed restrictions
  • Entities for holding property
  • Loan and lien documents

Tract 2

Residential

For people buying, selling, or transferring a home or a lot. A contract and a deed are short documents with long consequences. The firm reads them before you sign and prepares the ones you need.

Typical matters

  • Contract review before you sign
  • Deeds and transfers of title
  • Seller-financed sales
  • Title problems found before closing
  • Lot, boundary, and access questions
  • Closing document review

Tract 3

Farm & ranch

Rural land raises questions a house in town never does: who holds the water and the minerals, where the easements run, and what happens to the agricultural valuation after a sale. The firm deals with them in the contract, before closing.

Typical matters

  • Farm and ranch purchase and sale contracts
  • Agricultural, grazing, and hunting leases
  • Pipeline, utility, and access easements
  • Water, mineral, and surface rights review
  • Agricultural valuation and rollback tax questions
  • Boundary surveys and fence lines

Related work

Land use and zoning

Business law

Banking and lending

Loan documents, liens, and collateral for lending secured by real estate.

How it works

Most matters follow the same four steps, whether the property is a storefront, a house, or a section of pasture.

  1. 1

    Tell us about the property

    Send the address or county, what you plan to do, and any deadline already running, such as an option period or a closing date.

  2. 2

    Agree on scope and fee

    You get the scope of the work and the fee arrangement, flat or hourly, before work starts.

  3. 3

    Review, draft, negotiate

    The firm reads the title work, the survey, and the contract, drafts what is missing, and negotiates the terms that need to change.

  4. 4

    Close and record

    Documents are signed, funds move through the title company, and the deed is recorded with the county clerk.

Questions

Do I need a lawyer to buy or sell property in Texas?

Texas does not require one, and many sales close with only real estate agents and a title company. People usually bring in a lawyer when the contract is not a standard form, the land is rural, title or access is unclear, or the seller is carrying the financing.

What should I send with my first request?

The property’s address or legal description, the contract or lease if there is one, the title commitment and survey if you have them, and any deadline you are working against.

What does a lawyer look for in a title commitment and a survey?

The commitment lists what the title company will not insure against (Schedule B) and what has to be cleared before closing (Schedule C). The survey shows where the boundaries, easements, and encroachments actually fall on the ground. Reading the two together is how problems are found before closing instead of after.

Does sending the form make the firm my lawyer?

No. An attorney-client relationship starts only after the firm checks for conflicts of interest and you sign an engagement agreement. Until then, leave confidential details out of your message.

Where does the firm work?

The office is in McAllen, in the Rio Grande Valley. A Texas law license covers the whole state, so the firm can take on property matters anywhere in Texas.
